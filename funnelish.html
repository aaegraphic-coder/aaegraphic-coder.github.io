Funnelish AI: custom funnels on demand, built on what already works

Funnelish is a UK-based funnel and checkout platform for e-commerce store owners. Store owners wanted funnels customised to their store that still worked well, so the design team customised them one by one and sometimes built new elements from scratch. Even after we made 100 templates in six months, custom requests kept coming. I designed Funnelish AI and led the research behind it: an AI builder grounded in Funnelish’s own data, so owners can generate the custom funnel they want themselves.

My roleSenior Product Designer
CompanyFunnelish, London (remote)
ScopeAI builder, template store, tokens, checkout
PeriodJun 2024 – Oct 2026
The result

Owners generate the custom funnel they want, without the design team building each one by hand. The AI starts from Funnelish’s own data on what works, and users grew from 845 to 3,000+ while I redesigned the store and templates.

My role

What I owned as Senior Product Designer

I was the Senior Product Designer on a remote, UK-based team. I owned the design of Funnelish AI from research to release, set up the design foundations it builds on, and mentored junior designers so the whole product stayed consistent while it grew.

The challenge

100 templates, and owners still wanted custom

Store owners on Funnelish build sales funnels: a landing page, a checkout and an upsell. Most started from a template, but every store wanted its funnel to look and work its own way, while still converting. In six months we made 100 templates, and custom requests still kept coming.

The store owner

Wants a funnel that fits their store and still works well. A template that is close is not enough, so they ask for changes.

The team

Each request was customised one by one, sometimes with new elements built from scratch, which pulled designers away from the rest of the roadmap.

The question I started withHow might every store owner get a custom funnel that still works well, without the design team building it by hand?
Research

Finding where AI could take over custom work

I led research into AI-assisted funnel building. Instead of asking how to make more templates, I asked what owners actually needed: a funnel that fits their store, built from what they already have and from what we know works. The research had to answer three questions:

  1. What do owners bring?

    Product descriptions, images and sometimes existing HTML. Any of these should be enough to start.

  2. Where does trust break?

    AI output that looks finished but can't be edited, or that appears without explanation, is hard to trust and hard to fix.

  3. What has to stay consistent?

    Generated pages must look and behave like pages built by hand, with the same components, styles and checkout.

Teaching the AI what already works

Instead of letting the AI work out good funnel design on its own, I broke funnels down into their building blocks, such as the checkout card. Using Funnelish’s internal data, I mapped which blocks and combinations store owners used most and which worked most efficiently. That map became the AI’s reference. When an owner generates a funnel, the AI starts from patterns that already work, then adapts them to current trends and the settings the owner asked for.

The flow

Describe, plan, build, publish, refine

  1. Describe the product

    The owner starts from a short prompt, and can add text, an image or existing HTML as a source.

  2. See the plan first

    Before building, the AI shows which pages it will create, such as a landing page, checkout and upsell.

  3. Pages build in place

    Each page appears on the canvas step by step, using the same components as the regular builder.

  4. Publish

    The funnel goes live with its own link, ready to take orders.

  5. Refine with AI

    Select any part and ask for a change, or use quick actions like "Punchier" and "Shorter".

Key decisions

Making AI output something people can trust and control

1. Start from what owners already have

Text, images or HTML all work as input, so nobody has to write a perfect brief first.

2. Plan before build

Showing the page plan first makes the AI predictable and lets the owner see what is coming.

3. Generate real components

The AI builds with the same components and design tokens as the builder, so every page stays editable and consistent.

4. Edit in context

Changes happen on the page itself, with small quick actions for the most common edits, instead of starting over.

5. Ground the AI in what already works

Rather than asking the AI to learn good funnel design from scratch, it starts from a map of the blocks and combinations store owners use most and that work most efficiently. Output follows proven patterns and current trends from the first try, then adapts to the owner’s settings.

Foundations

The system that makes AI output feel native

AI only feels trustworthy if what it makes looks like the rest of the product. Alongside the AI work, I strengthened the foundations it builds on:

Design tokens

I initiated Figma design tokens and standard patterns: button hierarchy, component states, tables and badges.

Template store

I redesigned the funnel store and template system so users could create template variations far more easily.

Builder workflows

Funnel stages and steps, image upload and optimisation with live preview, and archive and restore actions.

I also mentored junior designers to apply the tokens and patterns, keeping the dashboard, storefront and templates consistent.

Checkout

Smart Payment Routing: fewer lost sales at the last step

A funnel only works if the payment goes through. One declined card or a gateway outage can cost a sale. With Smart Payment Routing, store owners connect several gateways, set a priority order and rules per region, and when one gateway declines or times out, the payment retries on the next one automatically. I worked on making these rules clear to set up and easy to trust.

What I learned

AI is only as good as the system under it

The most important design work was not the prompt box. It was making the AI show its plan, build with real components and stay easy to edit. When AI output follows the same rules as everything else in the product, people treat it as a starting point they own, not a black box.
