top.legal: know if a contract is fair, right inside the document

top.legal is a Germany-based legal-tech platform where people review and agree on contracts. Reviewing used to mean comparing the contract between the parties by hand, with no AI help, and waiting for answers. I designed improvements to the AI contract assistant so it flags one-sided and non-compliant clauses directly inside the agreement and recommends a fair alternative.

My role

What I owned as Senior Product Designer

I joined top.legal as a contract Senior Product Designer, working remotely with a Germany-based team. I owned the design of the AI contract assistant improvements and the rework for long German text, from concept to build, together with international engineers and stakeholders.

The challenge

Is this contract fair? Nobody could tell quickly

When two or more parties negotiated a contract, each side compared the clauses and changes by hand. There was no AI help. To find out whether a clause was fair, people had to read everything line by line, or wait for a lawyer or the other party to respond. Long German contracts made this even slower.

The person reviewing

Has to read every clause to find the few that matter, and can still miss a one-sided one.

The deal

Every round of waiting for review slows down the agreement for everyone involved.

The question I started withHow might people know whether a contract is fair in minutes, right where they read it, instead of comparing clauses by hand and waiting for answers?
Approach

From comparing by hand to an AI recommendation

The goal was not to replace the person’s judgement. It was to remove the slow part: finding the clauses that need attention. The AI reads the whole agreement, points to the risky clauses and recommends a fairer version. The person still reviews and decides.

The flow

Scan, flag, explain, decide

  1. Scan the agreement

    The assistant reviews every clause of the contract at once.

  2. Flag in context

    One-sided or non-compliant clauses are marked right inside the document, where the person is already reading.

  3. Explain and recommend

    Each flag says why the clause is a problem and suggests a fairer alternative.

  4. The person decides

    Accept the suggestion or edit it. Nothing changes without a human choice.

Key decisions

Making an AI recommendation easy to trust

1. Inside the agreement

Flags live in the document, not in a separate report, so people never lose their place.

2. Say why, not just what

Every flag explains what makes the clause one-sided or non-compliant.

3. Recommend, don’t overwrite

The AI suggests a fairer version, and the person accepts or edits it.

4. Focus on what matters

Instead of a long list, the clauses that need attention stand out first.

Long German text

Designing for the longest words in Europe

German legal terms are long, and the product had layouts that broke with them: truncated labels, overflowing buttons and cramped tables. I reworked layouts and typography so long German strings stay readable across screens, and delivered responsive, accessible UI with international engineering and stakeholder teams, from concept to build.

No truncation

Labels and headings wrap cleanly instead of being cut off.

No overflow

Buttons, tables and panels grow with the text instead of breaking.

Responsive

The same rules hold on large screens and small ones.

Outcome

A recommendation instead of a waiting game

With the assistant, people no longer had to compare a contract clause by clause or wait for someone else to review it. They could see right away whether a contract was fair, which clauses needed attention, and what a fairer version would look like, then decide for themselves.

What I learned

In legal work, AI earns trust by explaining itself

People reading a contract are careful by nature. A flag without a reason is just noise. The design work that mattered most was making every recommendation explain itself and leaving the final decision with the person.
